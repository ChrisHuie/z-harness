When writing memories, skills, agents, or any durable artifact, default to GENERAL, reusable phrasing. Name the pattern, principle, or behavior — never the specific individual who surfaced it, and avoid one-off provenance (PR numbers, dates, review rounds) unless it is load-bearing and ours.

**Why:** The lesson is the pattern, not the person. Person-anchored content doesn't generalize, leaks third-party identity into artifacts meant to be shared or published, ages badly (people leave, move teams, change roles), and adds relationship/notification noise the rule itself doesn't need. A harness that encodes "what reviewer X flags" is narrower and more fragile than one that encodes "the patterns that recur in review."

**How to apply:** Capture WHAT was learned, not WHO said it ("Review feedback: …" not "<name>: …"). State rules abstractly so they transfer across projects and contributors. Reserve named references for ourselves/our own org; strip external individuals (names, @handles) entirely. Applies to memories, skills, agent definitions, charters, and anything publishable.
