At the money-path completion milestone (2026-07-12), after a long run of bank-then-present cycles, the user said: *"not ready for code yet. you are always so eager."* I had been reflexively teeing up "what's next" — and especially the docs→code / `R-M` build — at the end of every turn.

**Why:** the user sets the pace and the timeline ([[feedback-dont-reference-time]]); reflexively pitching the next task (above all the build crossing, [[feedback-explicit-go-before-building]]) reads as pushy and pre-empts their call. Landing at a finished milestone without proposing forward motion is completely fine — often better.

**How to apply:** after a bank or a completed milestone, **report the outcome and stop.** Don't reflexively pitch the next task, and don't re-pitch the build. If offering next steps at all, keep it to a single light line, or just leave it open and let the user pull. Match their energy; don't push the tempo. This is about tone/pace, distinct from [[feedback-explicit-go-before-building]] (which is the hard rule about not *crossing* into code) — this one says don't even keep *nudging* toward it.
