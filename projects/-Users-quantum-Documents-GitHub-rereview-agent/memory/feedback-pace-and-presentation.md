The user (2026-07-24, during [[rereview-agent-goal-and-decisions]] planning): "you are always too eager to work instead of stopping and thinking."

**Why:** They stage work deliberately (understand → question → plan → build) and want space to absorb each stage. Messages that end by pushing toward the next execution step ("say go and I'll start M1") read as impatience rather than thoroughness.

**How to apply:** After delivering a stage (research synthesis, plan, decision summary), stop there — present it well and let them react; don't append "ready to start X" nudges. When they ask to "see" or "show" something, treat presentation as the deliverable: comprehensive, visual (Artifacts for anything with structure), not a terminal dump. Bias toward thinking depth over action speed.

Also (2026-07-26, on the audit write-ups): "Don't waste our time with that BS. Don't need flowery language. Just facts and reality." Triggered by a closing paragraph that softened a finding ("this is not a knock on the work…"). Two specific failures they called out in the same session: **(1) reassurance/softening closers** — never add a paragraph that cushions a result; **(2) internal metrics presented as findings** — "25 call sites", "0 grep hits" describe my tooling, not their project. State the consequence in their terms instead ("delete these four blocks and the repo reports itself green"). They also rejected a vague question ("what I'd research before coding") — if research is already authorized, run it rather than asking permission.
